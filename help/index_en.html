Coastline Dynamics

A QGIS plugin: annual shorelines and rates of their change from long Landsat and Sentinel-2 time series. The method follows Digital Earth Australia Coastlines (Bishop-Taylor et al., 2021).

Workflow

The calculation has five steps. Each step is a separate Processing algorithm (the “Coastline Dynamics” group in the Processing Toolbox); it is also available as a button on the plugin toolbar and in the menu “Plugins – Coastline Dynamics”. Each step takes the result of the previous one as input.

StepWhat it doesResult
1. Download scenes from the STAC catalogue Finds the scenes over the area in the Microsoft Planetary Computer catalogue and saves clips of three reflectance bands; clouds, shadows and snow are masked. Archive folder: one file per acquisition day and a scenes.csv table.
2. Annual composites of the water index From all images of the year computes the per-pixel median of the MNDWI or NDWI index, its standard deviation and the number of observations; builds fallback three-year composites. Composites folder: mndwi_YYYY.tif, mndwi_YYYY_gapfill.tif, rgb_YYYY.tif.
3. Annual shorelines Separates the sea from inland waters, restricts the analysis to a coastal strip and draws the shoreline of every year at sub-pixel precision. Line layer: fields year, certainty.
4. Rates of shoreline change At points every 30 m along the shoreline of the baseline year measures the position of every year's shoreline and fits a linear trend. Point layer: rate rate_time, significance sig_time, positions dist_YYYY.
5. Summarised rates along the coast Reduces point rates to overview points: the median within a circle of a given radius. Point layer for small scales.

The “Full run (steps 1–5)” algorithm runs the whole chain in one go with default parameters. Algorithm “2a” replaces steps 1 and 2 when annual reflectance composites already exist.

A repeated run of step 1 with the same folder fetches only the missing scenes. No account in the catalogue is needed.

Reading the result

  • Rate (rate_time, m/yr): negative means the coast is retreating, positive means it is growing.
  • Significance (sig_time): the probability of getting such a slope by chance. The trend is treated as established at 0.01 or less.
  • Certainty (certainty): good or the reason for doubt.
  • On the map, large symbols mark points where both certainty = good and sig_time ≤ 0.01; red tones mean retreat, blue tones mean growth.
  • Position (dist_YYYY, m): where the year's shoreline lay relative to the shoreline of the baseline year; positive is seaward, negative is landward.

Point chart

The “Shoreline change chart for a point” button opens a panel. Click “Pick a point on the map” and then a point of the rates layer: the panel shows the shoreline positions by year, the trend line and a summary. Hollow circles are years excluded from the trend as outliers.

Frequent questions

  • The sea freezes. Restrict the season to ice-free months (the parameters “First month of the season” and “Last month of the season” at steps 1 and 2).
  • The area contains a lagoon or estuary. By default the sea is the largest water body in the area. To point out the sea explicitly, create a layer with a point in the sea and set it in the parameter “Sea layer”.
  • Shores of a lake or reservoir are needed. At step 3 choose “All water bodies wider than 300 m” and turn off the exclusion of narrow watercourses in the advanced parameters.
  • A tidal sea. At step 2 give a sea level table (CSV: UTC date and time; level): only images taken at a level close to the mean will be used.
  • The coast changes quickly and in jumps. At step 4, in the advanced parameters, choose the exclusion of outlier years “By trend residuals” and check against the point chart: a linear trend describes gradual change, not one-off events.
